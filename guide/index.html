How to play

14

Prices, bet slips, Standard vs Rambolito, System Play, Lucky Pick, odds and prize tables for every PCSO game we cover.

  • How to Play Swertres (3D Lotto): Price, Rambolito, Prizes and OddsSwertres (3D Lotto) costs ₱10 per play: pick three digits from 0 to 9, choose Standard or Rambolito, and pick a draw at 2PM, 5PM or 9PM. Standard pays ₱4,500; Rambolito pays ₱1,500 or ₱750.
  • How to Play EZ2 (2D Lotto): Price, Rambolito, Prizes and OddsEZ2 (2D Lotto) costs ₱10 per play: pick two numbers from 1 to 31, choose Standard or Rambolito, and pick a 2PM, 5PM or 9PM draw. Standard pays ₱4,000; Rambolito pays ₱2,000.
  • How to Play Ultra Lotto 6/58: Price, System Play, Prizes and OddsUltra Lotto 6/58: pick six numbers from 1 to 58 for ₱25 per combination, drawn Tuesday, Friday and Sunday at 9PM. Minimum jackpot ₱75,000,000; jackpot odds 1 in 40,475,358.
  • How to Play Grand Lotto 6/55: Price, System Play, Prizes and OddsGrand Lotto 6/55: pick six numbers from 1 to 55 for ₱25 per combination, drawn Monday, Wednesday and Saturday at 9PM. Minimum jackpot ₱45,000,000; jackpot odds 1 in 28,989,675.
  • How to Play Super Lotto 6/49: Price, System Play, Prizes and OddsSuper Lotto 6/49: pick six numbers from 1 to 49 for ₱25 per combination, drawn Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday at 9PM. Minimum jackpot ₱25,000,000; jackpot odds 1 in 13,983,816.
  • How to Play Mega Lotto 6/45: Price, System Play, Prizes and OddsMega Lotto 6/45: pick six numbers from 1 to 45 for ₱25 per combination, drawn Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 9PM. Minimum jackpot ₱15,000,000; jackpot odds 1 in 8,145,060.
  • How to Play Lotto 6/42: Price, System Play, Prizes and OddsLotto 6/42: pick six numbers from 1 to 42 for ₱25 per combination, drawn Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday at 9PM. Minimum jackpot ₱10,000,000; jackpot odds 1 in 5,245,786, the best of the six-number games.
  • How to Play 4D Lotto: Price, Perm, Roll, Prizes and Odds4D Lotto costs ₱10 per combination: pick four digits from 0 to 9 for the 9PM draw on Monday, Wednesday or Friday. First prize starts at ₱10,000; the last three digits pay ₱800 and the last two ₱100.
  • How to Play 6D Lotto: Price, Prizes, Odds and Rollover6D Lotto costs ₱10 per combination: pick one digit in each of six columns for the 9PM draw on Tuesday, Thursday or Saturday. First prize starts at ₱150,000 and lower prizes pay from ₱40 to ₱40,000.
  • How Much Is a Lotto Ticket in the Philippines? PCSO Prices by GameSwertres, EZ2, 4D and 6D cost ₱10 per play. The six-number games (6/42, 6/45, 6/49, 6/55, 6/58) cost ₱25 per combination (checked on PCSO's game pages, 5 November 2026), up from ₱20; this price is pending verification. Verify at the outlet.
  • Can I Buy PCSO Lotto Online? e-Lotto Status and the Risks of 'Online Taya'No. PCSO's e-Lotto stopped selling on 11 July 2024, its supplier announced withdrawal in February 2026, and there is no official relaunch. Facebook 'online taya' pages are unlicensed. Buy only at authorized PCSO outlets.
  • Standard vs Rambolito: Which Swertres and EZ2 Play Type Should You Choose?Standard wins only in exact order and pays ₱4,500 (Swertres) or ₱4,000 (EZ2) per ₱10. Rambolito wins in any order and pays ₱750 or ₱1,500 (Swertres) or ₱2,000 (EZ2). Same expected value; different frequency of wins.
  • PCSO System Play Explained: System 5-Roll to System 12, Combinations and CostSystem Play lets you pick 7 to 12 numbers (or 5 plus a roll) and plays every six-number combination inside them. System 7 is 7 combinations (₱175 at ₱25); System 12 is 924 (₱23,100). It buys more tickets; it does not change any ticket's odds.
  • Lucky Pick (LP) Explained: What the PCSO Terminal Actually DoesLucky Pick lets the PCSO terminal choose your numbers at random for the same price and the same odds as numbers you pick yourself. It is available on every game. It is neither luckier nor unluckier than your own picks.

Claiming & tax

5

Where to claim by prize bracket, what to bring, the one-year deadline, and the 20% final tax.

  • How to Claim a PCSO Lotto Prize: Where to Go by Amount, Requirements and DeadlinePrizes of ₱10,000 and below: any outlet or PCSO branch. Above ₱10,000 to ₱300,000: PCSO branch office in your area (or the Main Office). Above ₱300,000: PCSO Main Office in Mandaluyong, paid by check. Bring the signed ticket, two valid IDs and the Winner's Profile Form within one year.
  • Lotto Tax in the Philippines: 20% on Prizes Above ₱10,000, With ExamplesPCSO prizes above ₱10,000 are subject to a 20% final tax on the full amount under the TRAIN Law. ₱20,000 becomes ₱16,000; ₱1,000,000 becomes ₱800,000. Prizes of ₱10,000 and below are not taxed.
  • Expired or Lost Lotto Ticket: The One-Year Rule and What PCSO AllowsA PCSO prize must be claimed within one year of the draw date or it is forfeited. PCSO requires the original ticket, so a lost ticket generally cannot be claimed. A damaged ticket may be validated if the barcode still reads.
  • Splitting a Lotto Jackpot With a Group: Written Agreement, Claiming and TaxA group ticket is paid to whoever presents it, so agree in writing beforehand who contributed and how shares are split. PCSO applies the 20% final tax once, to the prize on the ticket; the group divides the net amount.

Glossary

10

One-paragraph definitions. Each entry points you to the right page instead of a "tip".

  • EZ2The common name for PCSO's 2D Lotto: pick two numbers from 1 to 31 for ₱10, drawn daily at 2PM, 5PM and 9PM; Standard pays ₱4,000, Rambolito ₱2,000.
  • HearingPlayer slang for a Swertres or EZ2 combination someone claims to have 'heard' will come out. Not official, not information, and no effect on the odds; we explain it and point you to verified results instead.
  • Last 2An illegal numbers game under RA 9287 that uses the last two digits of official PCSO lotto results as its winning number. It is not a PCSO game; this site does not provide Last 2 results, odds or any related tool.
  • Lucky Pick (LP)The option to let the PCSO terminal choose your numbers at random, on any game, for the same price and the same odds as numbers you choose yourself.
  • PasakaySlang for 'riding along': betting the same numbers another player, page or group is backing. A social habit, not a method; it does not change the odds of the combination.
  • ProbablesLists of candidate Swertres or EZ2 combinations that a page or group believes are 'likely' to come out, usually built from recent results, angles or dream numbers. Guesses, not forecasts; the odds are unchanged.
  • RambolitoA play type in Swertres and EZ2 that wins if your numbers come out in any order, for a smaller prize than Standard: ₱750 or ₱1,500 in Swertres, ₱2,000 in EZ2.
  • Standard playThe default play type: in Swertres and EZ2 your digits must match in exact order (₱4,500 and ₱4,000 per ₱10); in the six-number games it means one ordinary six-number combination.
  • SwertresThe everyday name for PCSO's 3D Lotto: pick three digits from 0 to 9 for ₱10, drawn daily at 2PM, 5PM and 9PM; Standard pays ₱4,500.
  • System PlayAn option on the six-number games to pick 7 to 12 numbers (or 5 plus a roll) and play every six-number combination inside them; System 7 is 7 combinations, System 12 is 924. More tickets, same odds per ticket.

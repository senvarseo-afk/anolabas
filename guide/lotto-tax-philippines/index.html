Lotto Tax in the Philippines: 20% on Prizes Above ₱10,000, With Examples

PCSO prizes above ₱10,000 are subject to a 20% final tax on the full amount under the TRAIN Law. ₱20,000 becomes ₱16,000; ₱1,000,000 becomes ₱800,000. Prizes of ₱10,000 and below are not taxed.

How much tax is deducted from lotto winnings?

PCSO lotto prizes above ₱10,000 are subject to a 20% final tax on the full amount under the TRAIN Law (Republic Act 10963), as applied in the PCSO Citizen’s Charter 2025. Prizes of ₱10,000 and below are not taxed. The tax is withheld by PCSO when it pays you, so what you receive is already net; you do not file or pay anything further on that prize. Enter any amount in the tax calculator to see the take-home figure.

Mahigit ₱10,000 ang premyo? 20% ang kaltas sa buong halaga. ₱10,000 pababa, walang tax.

Is the 20% applied to the whole prize or only the excess over ₱10,000?

To the whole prize. This is the most common misunderstanding. The ₱10,000 figure is a threshold that decides whether the tax applies, not an exemption that is subtracted first. A ₱20,000 prize is taxed 20% of ₱20,000, which is ₱4,000, leaving ₱16,000. It is not 20% of the ₱10,000 excess.

Note the cliff: a ₱10,001 prize pays less in hand than a ₱10,000 prize. That is how the rule is written, and PCSO applies it as written.

Why are lotto prizes taxed, and since when?

Before 2018, PCSO lotto winnings were exempt from income tax. The TRAIN Law, effective 1 January 2018, removed that exemption for PCSO and lotto winnings above ₱10,000 and placed them under the 20% final tax that already applied to other prizes and winnings. “Final” means the tax is withheld at source and settles your liability on that income; the prize is not added to your salary for income tax purposes.

What are some worked examples?

The arithmetic is always the same: multiply the prize by 0.80 if it is above ₱10,000; otherwise keep the whole prize.

  1. Swertres Standard, ₱10 play: ₱4,500 prize, below the threshold, take-home ₱4,500.
  2. Swertres Standard, ₱50 play: ₱22,500 prize, above the threshold, tax ₱4,500, take-home ₱18,000.
  3. 6/42 lower-tier prize of ₱20,000: tax ₱4,000, take-home ₱16,000.
  4. ₱1,000,000 prize: tax ₱200,000, take-home ₱800,000.
  5. ₱100,000,000 jackpot shared by two tickets: each ticket wins ₱50,000,000; each is taxed ₱10,000,000; each receives ₱40,000,000.

Jackpot na ₱100 million, dalawang nanalo: ₱50 million bawat isa, ₱40 million ang uuwi.

Does the tax change where I claim?

Indirectly. The claiming brackets in PCSO’s FAQ and the Citizen’s Charter 2025 are set on the gross prize: up to ₱10,000 at any outlet (no tax), above ₱10,000 to ₱300,000 at a PCSO branch office or the Main Office, above ₱300,000 at the Main Office only; the Main Office pays cash up to ₱60,000 gross and by check above that. So a ₱60,000 prize can be claimed at a branch office and nets ₱48,000 after tax. Details are in how to claim.

Is a group or shared jackpot taxed once or per person?

The 20% final tax is applied to the prize paid on each winning ticket. If one ticket wins ₱100,000,000 and five friends share it under a private agreement, PCSO pays the ticket holder ₱80,000,000 after tax, and the group divides that net amount among themselves. The tax is not charged again when the holder passes shares to the others, although large transfers can raise separate questions about donor’s tax, which is a matter for a tax professional. See splitting a jackpot with a group.

Do I need to declare lotto winnings on my income tax return?

For prizes above ₱10,000, the 20% withheld by PCSO is a final tax, so the winnings are not included again in your annual income tax computation. Prizes of ₱10,000 and below from PCSO are exempt. Keep the acknowledgement PCSO gives you when you claim. This guide is general information drawn from PCSO’s published rules; for your own situation, especially large prizes or business-related questions, consult the BIR or a licensed accountant.

Final tax na ‘yan. Hindi na idadagdag sa income tax mo ang premyo.
